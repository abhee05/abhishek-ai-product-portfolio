01

Executive Summary

Compliance teams in Indian financial firms answer a constant stream of questions like "what's the current KYC review period?" or "can we rely on an external KYC audit?". Each answer requires cross-checking a shifting set of RBI regulations — a master direction dating back to 2016, recent 2024 and 2025 amendments — against the firm's own internal policies, and then producing a reply the business can act on with confidence.

Regulatory Intelligence Copilot is an AI-assisted regulatory research product built for that job. The MVP focuses on RBI KYC requirements and pairs the regulatory corpus with simulated internal policies so answers are grounded and traceable rather than fluent and expendable.

The product was designed, built and evaluated end-to-end. Retrieval quality improved from a baseline Hit@1 of 0.718 to 0.872 and MRR from 0.843 to 0.927 through focused iterations on freshness, source routing and evidence selection. Generation evaluation was partially completed due to API quota constraints — an outcome reported transparently rather than papered over.

Scope note

Internal policy documents used in this portfolio product are simulated stand-ins, clearly labeled as such, because a real NBFC would never expose proprietary policies. Regulatory sources are the genuine, current RBI materials.

02

Problem

Regulatory research in India is fragmented across documents that are constantly in motion. A compliance question touches the 2016 RBI Master Direction, the November 2024 amendment, the June 2025 amendment, published FAQs, and the firm's own internal policy — which must itself be evaluated against all of the above.

The result: answers are slow, inconsistently sourced, and hard to defend. When a compliance analyst replies to the business, what they are really selling is confidence. Today that confidence is built by hand, one Ctrl-F at a time.

The problem in one line

Compliance teams can't reliably find the current, applicable, authoritative regulatory position — with proof — fast enough to serve the business.

03

Target User

Compliance Analyst / Compliance Manager at an Indian NBFC (Non-Banking Financial Company).

They are the research layer of the compliance function. Business and product teams come to them with questions; regulators expect the firm to be able to demonstrate why a given practice is compliant. The analyst is measured on the correctness and traceability of the answers they give, not their speed alone.

They are time-starved, skeptical of anything they can't verify, and accountable for getting it right. Any tool that answers must also show its work.

04

Jobs To Be Done

Core JTBD

"When I receive a compliance-related business query, I want to quickly identify the current applicable regulatory and internal requirements, along with their authoritative sources, so that I can provide a reliable and traceable response."

Sub-jobs the product supports:

  • Confirm what regulation says today, including recent amendments.
  • Decide whether internal policy alone is sufficient or whether regulation supersedes it.
  • Produce an answer with the exact provision(s) cited — ready to paste into a reply.
  • Know when the documents genuinely don't answer the question.

Notably, the measure of success is a traceable answer, not a chatbot conversation. That shapes every downstream product decision.

05

Product Discovery

Discovery followed a JTBD lens: start from the job, back into the information flows that serve it, and pressure-test where AI adds durable value versus where it's decoration.

  • Mapped the domain. RBI KYC/Customer Due Diligence rules were read and structured as a small knowledge graph: master direction, amendments, FAQs, and the points where each layer supersedes or clarifies the other.
  • Studied the amendment lifecycle. The Nov 2024 and Jun 2025 amendments don't replace the 2016 master direction — they layer over it. Knowing which document "wins" on a given topic is the core of the analyst's craft, and the hardest part to automate correctly.
  • Framed the internal/regulatory boundary. Firms operationalize RBI rules through their own policies. The true job is often "what does the firm require, and is it compliant with what RBI requires" — a two-layer question.
  • Studied evaluation as a first-class concern. This is an answer-quality problem, so success criteria (retrieval metrics, citation accuracy) were defined before building, not after.
Full transparency

Regulatory Intelligence Copilot is an independent AI product portfolio project. It is never presented as Deloitte, Wipro, HCL or client work. Discovery drew on public RBI materials, the compliance workflow narrative common across Indian financial services, and the product principles below — and stands in for a live-enterprise discovery. Internal documents are simulated stand-ins, as flagged throughout.

06

Current Compliance Workflow

  1. A query arrives. Business/product asks Compliance a question, typically in chat or email.
  2. Manual search. The analyst opens the RBI master direction, amendment PDFs and FAQs and searches by keyword.
  3. Cross-document reconciliation. They piece together which provision is current after each amendment — the error-prone step.
  4. Internal policy check. They pull the firm's KYC policy and judge whether it operationalizes, tightens or conflicts with regulation.
  5. Draft answer. An answer is drafted, typically with the provision(s) cited by section number.
  6. Review. A senior compliance member sanity-checks before the reply goes out.

Speed is bounded by documents, not expertise. The analyst is competent; the process is not.

07

Key Pain Points

P1

It's hard to know what's current

Amendments stack on a 2016 master direction. Without careful tracking, an analyst can quote superseded language.

P2

Sources are fragmented

Rules live across directions, amendments and FAQs — none of which a keyword search glues together into an answer.

P3

Answers need evidence

Every claim needs a citable provision. Winning the answer is wasted if you can't win the citation.

P4

Internal vs regulatory is murky

Analysts must reconcile firm policy with regulation — and say which one applies when they differ.

P5

Ambiguity burns time

Many queries are imprecise. Deciding what the question actually means is manual and slow.

P6

Quiet refusal is hard

When documents don't answer a question, analysts still have to say something — and "we don't know" is hard to staff.

08

Product Goal

Turn hours of cross-document research into a minutes-scale traceable answer — with the same (or better) confidence a senior analyst would demand, and a clear, honest "insufficient evidence" response when the corpus can't support an answer.

Goal statement

"Answer compliance research queries on RBI KYC requirements with grounded responses that cite the current, applicable and authoritative sources — and explicitly flag when the evidence is insufficient."

09

MVP Scope

The MVP deliberately confines itself to what proves the core job: grounded answers with citations on RBI KYC, plus simulated internal policies to exercise the two-layer case. Everything else is carved out on purpose.

In scope (MVP)

  • RBI KYC / Customer Due Diligence corpus (real regulatory docs)
  • Four simulated internal NBFC policies (labeled as such)
  • Grounded Q&A with citations for every claim
  • Evidence selection across regulatory and internal sources
  • Freshness/amendment-aware ranking
  • Insufficient-evidence behavior
  • An evaluation harness and golden-question dataset

Out of scope (by design)

  • Regulatory filing
  • Autonomous compliance decisions
  • Approval workflows
  • Authentication / RBAC
  • PII processing
  • User-uploaded documents
  • Multi-agent architecture

Each exclusion is a conscious product decision (see Key Product Decisions) — not an omission.

10

Product Requirements

Translated the goal into requirement statements, each independently testable:

  1. PR-01The system shall answer questions grounded in the ingested RBI KYC corpus and simulated internal policies only.
  2. PR-02Every substantive claim shall carry a citation resolving to an ingested source document and provision.
  3. PR-03Currentness shall be determined by document type and date, so that amendment language takes precedence over superseded text.
  4. PR-04The system shall separate regulatory from internal-policy evidence and disclose which is being cited.
  5. PR-05When no sufficient evidence exists, the system shall state that explicitly and not fabricate an answer.
  6. PR-06Simulated internal policies shall be unmistakably labeled as such in every surfaced result.
  7. PR-07Answers shall be research-support only, with a visible disclaimer that they are not a legal/compliance opinion.
  8. PR-08Evaluation shall measure retrieval and generation quality against a fixed golden-question dataset.

12

Solution

A retrieval-augmented question-answering product: a Streamlit web app backed by an embedding + vector store + LLM pipeline that retrieves grounded evidence, reasons over it, and returns answers with citations.

The design centers on four product principles applied to every feature decision:

Current

— the answer reflects the latest amendment state.

Applicable

— the right layer (regulatory vs internal) is surfaced for the question.

Authoritative

— answers only come from the corpus, never from the model's general knowledge.

Traceable

— every claim maps to a cited provision the analyst can open and verify.

The routing step (2) is where product thinking lives: not every question targets the same source set. Freshness-aware ranking and regulatory-vs-internal routing were the two highest-leverage product decisions in the stack (see Iterations).

14

Data Strategy

A small, curated corpus beats a large, messy one — especially where every sentence becomes a legal citation. The strategy was:

  • Curate before you embed. Only canonical regulatory documents and the simulated policies enter the store.
  • Keep provenance on every chunk. Source doc, document type, and (where applicable) issue date travel with the data so citations and freshness logic have something to work with.
  • Separate the layers. Regulatory vs internal sources stay distinguishable through the whole pipeline — this is non-negotiable given pain point P4.
  • Disclose the simulated part. Every simulated policy is labeled at ingestion and re-labeled at output.
Simulated data disclosure

The four internal policy documents are simulated NBFC policies created for this portfolio product. They are not views of any real firm's policy. Each is labeled SIMULATED INTERNAL POLICY — CREATED FOR PORTFOLIO PRODUCT in the app and in documentation.

15

Regulatory Sources

The authoritative regulatory corpus for the MVP:

  • RegRBI Master Direction — Know Your Customer (KYC) Directions, 2016
    The foundation document defining KYC/SLM, risk-based CCA, and customer due diligence.
  • RegKYC Amendment, November 2024
    Recent changes — e.g. updated risk-based approach and digital KYC provisions.
  • RegKYC Amendment, June 2025
    The latest layer — the most current position on amended provisions.
  • RegRBI KYC FAQs
    Reserve Bank guidance-style clarifications that operationalize the directions.

These are the real RBI documents. Having the genuine regulations — not paraphrases — is what makes the product authoritative by construction.

16

Simulated Internal Policies

Four simulated NBFC policies exercise the all-important regulatory-vs-internal reconciliation:

  • Internal*Customer Due Diligence Policy (Simulated)
    Operationalizes CDD steps, onboarding docs and risk categories.
  • Internal*Risk-Based Approach & Client Risk Categorization Policy (Simulated)
    Turns RBI's RBA into the firm's risk tiers and triggers.
  • Internal*KYA / Ongoing Monitoring Policy (Simulated)
    Refresh, review and transactional monitoring obligations.
  • Internal*Enhanced Due Diligence / PEP Policy (Simulated)
    EDD escalations, PEP treatment and additional scrutiny.

* SIMULATED INTERNAL POLICY — CREATED FOR PORTFOLIO PRODUCT

The policies are crafted to clash and overlap with regulation in realistic ways, so the pipeline is forced to handle the genuinely hard case: "policy says X, regulation says Y — which governs?"

17

Retrieval & Generation Approach

Retrieval

  • Source routing. The question is classified as regulatory, internal-policy, or compare-the-two, and the search set is scoped accordingly.
  • Embedding. BAAI/bge-base-en-v1.5 encodes queries and chunks into the same space.
  • Vector search. Chroma returns dense-similarity candidates.
  • Evidence selection. Candidates are re-ranked and validated — bearing amendments and citation quality in mind.

Generation

  • Grounded decoding. The Groq-hosted openai/gpt-oss-120b model answers from the evidence only.
  • Citation binding. Each claim is written to reference its supporting provision; citations are validated against retrieval before surfacing.
  • Insufficiency behavior. If evidence is thin or missing, the model is instructed to say so rather than improvise.

The product decision embedded here: retrieval is the product. The LLM is deliberately interchangeable middleware. That is why the evaluation and iteration effort concentrated on retrieval quality.

18

AI Evaluation Strategy

You can't iterate what you can't measure. The evaluation harness was built from a golden-question dataset of approximately 50 questions designed to stress the real failure modes of this domain:

Case

Direct retrieval

Simple, one-document questions — the happy path must be perfect.

Case

Multi-document

Answers assembled across several sources at once.

Case

Internal vs regulatory

Reconciliation questions that must pick the governing layer.

Case

Amendment / freshness

Questions where the answer changed in Nov 2024 or Jun 2025 — the hardest, most business-critical set.

Case

Ambiguous

Under-specified queries that demand clarification or a scoped reading.

Case

Insufficient evidence

Queries the corpus genuinely cannot answer — the system must refuse gracefully.

Case

Adversarial

Leading, trap, or out-of-domain questions testing whether the model stays grounded.

Metrics chosen: Hit@1, Hit@3, Hit@5, Recall@5, MRR for retrieval; groundedness/citation accuracy and refusal correctness for generation.

19

Baseline Evaluation

A naive "embed everything, retrieve top-k, answer" baseline produced solid but not trustworthy numbers:

Hit@1 0.718
Hit@3 0.974
Hit@5 1.000
Recall@5 0.985
MRR 0.843

The headline: top-5 retrieval nearly always contains the right evidence, but the right evidence only ranks first in ~72% of cases. For a compliance answer, being buried at rank 3 is a product failure, because the user isn't going to read three candidate clauses to find the right one.

20

Failure Analysis

Reading the misses case-by-case revealed four systematic failure themes — not random noise:

F1

Freshness blindness

Semantically similar provisions across the 2016 and 2025 documents confused ranking — the amendment isn't later in embedding space, it's later in time.

F2

Layer confusion

Internal-policy chunks and regulatory chunks look alike to a dense retriever, so "what does our policy require" pulled regulatory text, and vice-versa.

F3

Shallow evidence

"Good enough" chunks were retrieved even when a better, more specific clause existed deeper in a document.

F4

Citation mismatch

Occasionally the right provision was retrieved but a weaker source was cited in the answer — eroding traceability.

The analytical takeaway

None of these are model failures; they are product failures. Dense similarity alone can't see recency, source type, or "authority" — so those had to be engineered into the retrieval design.

21

Product Iterations

Each failure theme generated a targeted, measured change:

  • Amendment / freshness-aware ranking. Retrieval now boosts later-dated documents where provisions overlap, fixing F1.
  • Source-type routing. Questions are classified into regulatory / internal / compare buckets before retrieval, fixing F2.
  • Better evidence selection. Post-retrieval re-ranking prefers the most specific, on-topic clause over a generic match, improving on F3.
  • Citation precision improvements. Citation binding is validated against retrieved evidence so answers cite the provision they actually rely on, fixing F4.
  • Retrieval-depth improvements. Wider candidate recall before selection reduces the chance the right clause never makes the shortlist.

Every change was A/B'd against the same golden set — no change shipped without numbers attached.

23

AI Guardrails

Guardrails are product requirements here, not bolt-on safety theater. They follow directly from PR-01 through PR-08:

G1

Evidence-grounded generation

The model may only answer from retrieved corpus evidence — never from parametric memory.

G2

Citation validation

Every citation is checked against the retrieved evidence before it's shown; mismatches are dropped or flagged.

G3

Explicit insufficient-evidence behavior

No evidence → explicit "insufficient evidence" statement, not a guess.

G4

Source-type separation

Regulatory and internal evidence are never conflated in one answer.

G5

Simulated-policy disclosure

Simulated policies carry persistent, unmistakeable labels.

G6

Research-support disclaimer

Outputs are research support, not a legal or compliance opinion.

G7

No autonomous compliance decisions

The tool informs the human; it never adjudicates compliance by itself.

Risks & Mitigations

RiskMitigation
Hallucinated citations / provisions that don't exist Citation validation against retrieved evidence (G2); grounded generation only (G1).
Quoting superseded (2016) wording after an amendment Freshness-aware ranking (Iteration 1) + amendment-dated preservation of provenance.
Confusing internal policy with RBI regulation Source-type routing and hard label separation (G4); explicit layer disclosure per answer.
Users treating output as a final compliance decision Research-support disclaimer (G6), no autonomous decisions (G7), human-in-the-loop journey design.
Model/API availability or quota limits Interchangeable LLM layer; retrieval evaluated independently; quota-gated generation eval reported honestly.
Corpus drift as RBI issues new amendments Versioned corpus ingestion with dates; freshness logic reads doc recency — not just embedding similarity.

Success Metrics

Success is measured at two levels: does the system retrieve and answer correctly, and does the analyst work faster with equal confidence?

Retrieval Hit@1 ≥ 0.85 target · achieved 0.872
Retrieval MRR ≥ 0.90 target · achieved 0.927
Citation precision ≥ 0.95 validated at eval
Refusal correctness 100% insufficient-evidence cases

Pilot-stage metrics (future measurement, not claimed results)

  • Time-to-traceable-answer reduction vs. manual baseline.
  • % of queries answered without supervisor escalation.
  • Analyst verification rate — do citations get opened and trusted?
  • Business repeat queries — questions re-asked because the answer wasn't reliable.

I deliberately list pilot metrics as what would be measured, not as achieved numbers. A portfolio that fabricates engagement results is the reason enterprise buyers trust engineers over slideware.

    Key Product Decisions

    Decision · One domain first

    Scope the MVP to RBI KYC/Customer Due Diligence only, rather than "all financial regulation."

    WhyA curated, correct single-domain answer beats a broad, shallow one — and every architecture lesson generalizes afterward.
    Decision · Simulated internal policies

    Build four labeled stand-in policies instead of waiting on, or faking, real enterprise access.

    WhyIt lets the product exercise the internal-vs-regulatory case honestly — while disclosure keeps it credible.
    Decision · Retrieval-first evaluation

    Measure retrieval (Hit@k, MRR) as the primary quality gate, before judging generation.

    WhyGarbage-in is undetectable in a fluent answer; nailing retrieval fixes most quality issues at the root.
    Decision · Interchangeable LLM layer

    The model is chosen at a thin integration boundary — currently Groq hosting an open-weight model.

    WhyKeeps cost/availability/quota churn from becoming a product dependency.
    Decision · Explicit "insufficient evidence"

    Design refusals as a first-class, good answer — not a fallback for failures.

    WhyFor compliance, a clear "the corpus can't answer this" is a feature that builds more trust than a confident guess.
    Decision · No auth / no PII at MVP

    Ship without logins and without processing personal data in the MVP demo.

    WhyKeeps the security surface tiny while proving the core job; auth and privacy land with real users in Phase 4.

    MVP trade-offs were judged against one question: does this trade make the core JTBD more provable or less? If it didn't help the analyst's job, it wasn't worth the cost.

    29

    What I Learned

    • Embeddings don't know time. A 2025 regulation is not "closer" to a question than its 2016 ancestor in vector space. Recency had to be a first-class retrieval feature — the single biggest lesson from F1.
    • The LLM is the easiest part. Grounding, retrieval and evaluation dominate the engineering — and the product — effort. That inverts the naive "model-first" instinct.
    • Traceability is the feature. For compliance users, the citation is the answer. Language is just the carrier.
    • Refusals are product surface. "We don't know" is a designed moment, not an error state, and it earned the most user trust of any behavior.
    • Report the incomplete, or it will be assumed dishonest. The quota-limited generation evaluation got the same transparent treatment as every shipped number — that discipline is the product.
    • Evaluation datasets must encode business risk, not just "difficulty." Amendment and adversarial cases earn their place because they represent real-world failure costs.

    Future Opportunities

    • New regulatory domains — extend the same pipeline to AML/KYC outsourcing, payment regulation, or lending rules; the routing and freshness machinery ports directly.
    • Amendment watch — monitor RBI publications and flag which internal policies are affected when a new amendment drops.
    • Policy-impact mapping — given a regulation change, surface every internal policy that needs review.
    • Audit trail & workflow — record queries, citations and answers for the firm's compliance audit file (auth + logging land here).
    • Benchmark the copilot vs. a senior analyst — on real (consented) queries, measuring time and confidence, rather than demo anecdotes.
