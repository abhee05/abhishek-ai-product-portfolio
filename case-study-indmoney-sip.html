01

Product Overview

This project has two parts that answer one question. The discovery engine is an AI research tool: it collects public investor feedback, codes each record against the brief's questions, and computes in code which unmet needs matter most for first-year investors. Before you pause is the MVP that came out of it: a screen that sits between a worried tap on Pause or Stop and the decision itself.

Both parts follow the same rule: the AI reads and writes language; code does every count, score and calculation; and quality checks decide what reaches the user.

02

The Brief & Problem

A SIP only builds wealth if it keeps running. Many first-time investors start one, see their portfolio turn red or hit a tight month, and stop — often within the first year.

Goal

Raise 12-month SIP continuation for first-time mutual-fund investors on INDmoney. An active SIP means at least one successful instalment in the last 30 days.

The brief set constraints that shaped every later decision:

  • No fund or stock recommendations. The product may explain, never advise.
  • No fear, FOMO or urgency nudges. Continuation has to be earned with clarity, not pressure.
  • Nine research questions — why people start, what changes after, what triggers a pause or stop, how often they check, what confuses them in the app, where they go when unsure, what keeps continuers going, how segments differ, and which unmet needs matter most.

Answering nine questions by hand-reading forums would take weeks and would be impossible to audit. That is the job the discovery engine was built for.

03

How the Engine Works

1 Collect Public sources, no login; authors stored as one-way hashes
→
2 Prefilter Code dedupes and keeps SIP / mutual-fund records
→
3 AI coding LLM labels each record on an 18-field codebook
→
4 Quality gates Allowed values, exact quote, consistency; one retry, then rejected
→
5 Analysis Themes, segments and opportunity scores computed in code
→
6 Golden set 20 blind records I labelled by hand; agreement + Cohen's kappa
→
7 Ask the engine Grounded Q&A with exact-quote citations

The codebook

Each of the 18 fields maps to one of the brief's questions — for example drop_trigger (income shock, red portfolio, failed mandate, app confusion…), journey_stage, unmet_need and continuation_risk (0–3, how close the record is to a lapsed SIP). Segment fields such as age and income are filled only from explicit evidence; the model is told never to guess them.

The opportunity score

Each unmet need is ranked by reach × closeness to a lapse × first-year share × controllability. The first three come from the data; controllability is my product judgement of how much INDmoney can change it, and the weights can be adjusted in the app.

Ask the engine

Grounded Q&A over the coded evidence, with four visible gates: the question must be in scope (no advice), there must be enough evidence, every claim cites an exact quote, and every number comes from code.

Quality Gates & Golden Set

Every AI label had to pass code checks before it counted: only allowed values, an exact supporting quote from the record, and a journey stage consistent with the risk score. A label that failed got one retry, then was excluded.

Did the AI label like a researcher would?

Before looking at any AI output, I hand-labelled a blind, source-stratified sample of 20 records and compared field by field.

Product (which platform)80% · κ 0.68
Relevance55% · κ 0.39
Drop trigger55% · κ 0.22
Continuation risk55% · κ 0.24
Unmet need35% · κ −0.02
Journey stage20% · κ 0.14
Investor experience20% · κ −0.02

Overall field-level agreement was 46%, and only 2 of 20 records agreed on every field. The main failure pattern was caution: where I read implicit context (an experienced investor, an ongoing SIP, a need to understand volatility), the model often answered unknown or none.

What this result means

46% is not good enough to treat the engine's labels as measurement. So I used it for what it can do — rank where to look across thousands of records and surface exact quotes — and checked the leading need against six user interviews, a survey and a test of INDmoney's own assistant. Reporting the weak number is the point: it is what decided how much weight the engine's output could carry.

The insight that shaped the MVP

The biggest need (help when unsure) and the need closest to a lapse (a flexible pause, 47% of mentions at or near one) meet at a single moment: the tap on Pause or Stop. That is where a first-time investor is most in doubt and closest to leaving — and in my research, none of them got a useful explanation inside their app there. They went to YouTube, Reddit, colleagues or ChatGPT, or decided alone.

07

The MVP: Before You Pause

In my tests of INDmoney on 3 and 7 November 2026, its assistant answered "Should I stop my SIP?" with "I can't decide that for you" and then went straight to "Reply Yes to stop it". The stop screen asked why only after the SIP was stopped. Before you pause puts understanding before the decision:

  1. What's making you want to pause? Asked first, so the explanation fits the reason — "it's red", "money is tight" or "should I stop?".
  2. Where you stand. Invested amount, current value and return, calculated from real daily fund NAVs (AMFI data via mfapi.in).
  3. Is this normal? How every past SIP of the same length in the same fund looked at the same point — bad cases included — using only prices known at that time.
  4. Ask anything. Plain answers about the investor's own SIP. Fund picks and predictions are declined.
  5. Your options. Continue, skip once, lower the amount, pause until a chosen month, or stop. Stop is never hidden and never slowed down.

Where AI earns its place

A static chart or FAQ shows everyone the same thing. The AI reads why someone came, answers in their words and explains their numbers. Code does every calculation; the AI only writes sentences from those numbers; and code checks every sentence before it is shown.

08

Key Product Decisions

Decision · Intervene at the tap, not before it

The product appears when someone taps Pause or Stop, not as a general education feed.

WhyThe evidence put the strongest need and the highest lapse risk at the same moment. Help offered at any other time competes for attention; help at the tap answers a question the user is asking right now.
Decision · Code computes, AI only writes

Every number shown comes from code; any number in an AI sentence that the code did not produce blocks the answer.

WhyA wrong return figure in a money product destroys trust instantly. Language is where an LLM adds value; arithmetic is where it adds risk.
Decision · Show the bad cases too

Base rates include every past SIP of the same length, including ones that stayed below the amount invested.

WhyShowing only recoveries would be a nudge dressed as data — exactly what the brief ruled out. Honest base rates let the investor judge for themselves.
Decision · Decline advice and predictions

"Which fund?" and "buy, sell or switch?" point to a SEBI-registered adviser; "when will it recover?" gets past base rates instead of a forecast.

WhyThese are the questions users most want answered and the ones the product must never answer. A clear, useful decline is part of the product, not an error state.
Decision · Never hide Stop

All five options are always visible, and Stop is never slowed down or guilt-tripped.

WhyThe goal is a better-informed decision, not a retained user at any cost. Dark patterns would raise continuation briefly and erode trust permanently.
Decision · Template fallback

If the AI is unavailable or its draft fails the checks twice, a written template answer appears, labelled as such.

WhyThe screen sits in a money flow, so it must work every time. Degrading to a plain, correct answer is better than an empty or unchecked one.
09

MVP Scope

In scope

  • Reason-first entry when Pause or Stop is tapped
  • Real SIP maths on real daily fund NAVs
  • Historical base rates for the same fund and SIP length
  • Plain-language Q&A, including Hinglish questions
  • Routing that declines advice and predictions
  • Code checks on every AI answer, with template fallback
  • Five options: continue, skip once, lower, pause until, stop
  • "Not enough data to say" for funds with too little history

Out of scope (by design)

  • Fund or stock recommendations
  • Buy, sell or switch suggestions
  • Return forecasts or recovery dates
  • Fear, FOMO or urgency messaging
  • Linking a real INDmoney account — the prototype uses example SIPs on real fund prices
  • Executing a pause or stop — nothing real changes

Skip once and Pause until are proposed features; lowering the amount points to what INDmoney already offers inside Edit SIP.

MVP Evaluation

I built a golden set of 25 questions from real sources — the INDmoney assistant test, the six interviews, the survey and MVP user tests — plus edge cases I wrote to break it (Hinglish, a job loss, a fund with under a year of history, "tell me a joke"). Every expected behaviour was labelled by hand before scoring.

Cases passed 25 / 25
Answer correctly 12 / 12
Decline advice 6 / 6
Decline prediction 4 / 4
Safe fallback 3 / 3

In the 7 November run, 7 answers were written by the AI (openai/gpt-oss-120b on Groq) and none were blocked by the checks; the rest were routed to fixed answers or declines, as designed.

On what this result is

25 of 25 shows the product behaves as specified on the cases I could think of. It is a release check, not evidence that the screen raises continuation — that needs a live experiment.

11

Limitations

  • Public feedback over-represents complaints and vocal users; it is not a random sample of investors.
  • Segments are thin. First-time status, age and income are rarely stated, so segment groups are small.
  • Post dates are not tenure. The engine ranks where to look; it cannot measure retention rates.
  • Labels are imperfect. 46% golden-set agreement means engine counts are directional, not precise.
  • Controllability is judgement. The opportunity score's fourth factor is my estimate, and adjustable in the app.
What I am not claiming

No retention uplift, user numbers or adoption are claimed for this project. The MVP has not been tested in production, so there is no evidence base for any of them.

13

What I'd Test Next

Hypotheses — not built

Everything here is a hypothesis for a future version, not current functionality.

  • A controlled experiment on the Pause and Stop flow — Before you pause versus the current flow, measured on 12-month SIP continuation, with a guardrail on complaints and support contacts. This is the only way to learn whether the screen works.
  • Improve label agreement before scaling the engine — sharpen the codebook definitions behind the weakest fields (journey stage, investor experience, unmet need) and re-run the golden set.
  • Skip once and Pause until as real controls — the need closest to a lapse; worth testing whether a lighter option than Stop keeps the habit alive.

The point of this build

Using AI to do discovery at a scale one researcher can't, measuring honestly how far to trust it, and turning the strongest signal into a grounded product moment with guardrails I can test.
